"We have an ATS. We have recruiters. We have a process. Why isn't it working?"

This is the question TA leaders ask when their hiring metrics plateau: when time-to-fill creeps up, quality-of-hire stays flat, and recruiters feel buried in coordination instead of doing the work they're actually good at.

The answer is rarely the tool. It's rarely the recruiter. It's almost always the gap between strategy and system: and the absence of a professional whose job is to close that gap.

That's the role the TASSP framework was built to define, elevate, and equip. Not a new headcount category: a new professional identity that already exists inside most TA orgs, waiting to be recognized for what it actually is.

The Framework

What a TASSP Actually Does

The TASSP is the professional responsible for translating talent strategy into system capability. Five domains. One connective role.

01
⚙️
System Architecture Ownership
Designs and governs the recruiting tech ecosystem: ATS configuration, CRM, AI sourcing tools, scheduling systems, and cross-platform integrations.
02
🔁
Recruiting Process Engineering
Shapes how hiring actually flows: pipeline stage architecture, automation triggers, recruiter workflows, hiring manager processes, and candidate experience design.
03
📊
Data & Hiring Intelligence
Ensures leaders can answer: Where are we losing candidates? What stages create bottlenecks? What sources produce the best hires?
04
🧭
Technology Strategy
Helps decide what tools belong in the stack, when to consolidate platforms, how to implement AI responsibly, and how to integrate systems across the org.
05
🛡️
Operational Governance
Maintains standards that protect data integrity: naming conventions, data hygiene, process documentation, permissions, compliance, and audit readiness.

Who This Is For

Two Sides of the Same Problem

The TASSP framework speaks directly to two groups: and both need each other to succeed.

For TA Leaders & HR Executives

You have the strategy. You need someone to make the system match it.

TA Directors and VPs set hiring goals, define quality-of-hire standards, and report to the business on results. But strategy only executes as well as the systems that carry it. If your ATS is a data graveyard, your CRM isn't converting pipeline, or your recruiters are buried in manual work: the problem isn't strategy. It's the gap between strategy and system.

Time-to-fill creeping up with no clear data on why
Recruiting tech that's configured but not optimized
Recruiters spending time on coordination, not recruiting
Reports that describe what happened, not what to do next
