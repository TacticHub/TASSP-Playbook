The TASSP Onboarding Framework

Your Admin Was Set Up
to Figure It Out.
That Ends Here.

Most organizations hand a system admin the keys to the most complex ecosystem in HR and walk away. No roadmap. No context. No strategy. The TASSP Onboarding Framework is the structured 30/60/90 day path that changes that — whether your admin is brand new or has been in the seat for years.

"That's a failure of onboarding, not ability. A proper onboarding plan is the first step to building the next generation of TASSP leaders inside your org."
— From the TASSP Framework

The 30 / 60 / 90 Day Framework

Four Phases. One Strategic Path.

Each phase builds on the last. Pre-boarding sets the foundation. Day 30 builds confidence. Day 60 builds competence. Day 90 establishes strategic ownership.

Phase 0 · Before Day 1
Foundation
Stack mapping, access provisioning, and orienting the admin before they touch anything live.
Phase 1 · Days 1–30
Orientation
Learning the tools, the team, the workflows, and where the bodies are buried in the current config.
Phase 2 · Days 31–60
Competence
Connecting system knowledge to strategy. First independent optimizations, first metrics ownership.
Phase 3 · Days 61–90
Ownership
The admin presents a roadmap, owns a KPI dashboard, and graduates to full TASSP positioning.

Most admins walk in on day one with zero access and zero context. This phase ensures the leader has done the work before the admin even starts — and that the admin knows what they're walking into.

Already in the Seat

No Formal Onboarding? There's No Better Time to Start.

Most admins were never onboarded properly. That's not a failure — it's an industry-wide gap. The TASSP approach works at any stage of tenure. Here's how to retrofit it.

🗺️
Run the Stack Audit Anyway
Even if you've been in the role for 2 years, the audit in Phase 0 and Phase 1 is worth running. Most tenured admins discover integrations they didn't know existed, reports nobody reads, and automations that fire incorrectly.
Document every tool, integration, and workflow as if you're briefing your replacement
Flag any configuration decisions made without a documented rationale
Identify the last time any workflow was reviewed vs. just maintained
📊
Connect Your Work to the KPIs
If you can't answer these five questions from the system right now — time-to-fill, time-to-hire, offer acceptance rate, source of hire, stage conversion rate — that's your Phase 2 work. Start there.
Build one dashboard that answers a question leadership actually asks
Map one recent system change to a business outcome using the Triad
Present that connection to your leader, even informally
🎓
Get Certified if You Aren't
Certification isn't about proving you know the system. You already do. It's about having the credential that gives you standing to propose changes, push back on configurations, and influence how the stack evolves.
Identify available certifications for each platform you administer
Commit to completing at least one in the next 60 days
Use certification communities as a network, not just a training resource
