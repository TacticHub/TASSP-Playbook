Hiring Velocity
Time to Fill
How long it takes to move from approved requisition to accepted offer
+

Definition & Context

Time to Fill is the most widely tracked hiring velocity metric. It captures the total elapsed calendar days from the point a role is approved (or posted) to the point a candidate accepts an offer. This metric reflects the efficiency of your entire TA pipeline: sourcing, screening, interviewing, and offer stages all roll up here.

How organizations measure it

Job Approval → Offer Accepted Most conservative. Captures internal approval delays and full process duration.
Job Posted → Offer Accepted Common alternative. Excludes pre-posting admin time; focuses on candidate-facing activity.
First Application Received → Offer Accepted Pipeline-focused. Best for benchmarking sourcing quality and application volume impact.

Hiring Velocity
Time to Hire
How long it takes a candidate to move through your process from first contact to offer acceptance
+

Definition & Context

Often confused with Time to Fill, Time to Hire is candidate-centric rather than requisition-centric. It measures the candidate's experience of speed, measured from the moment they first engage with your process (applied, sourced, or referred) to when they sign. This metric helps identify where candidate friction lives.

How organizations measure it

Application Submitted → Offer Accepted Standard definition. Reflects total candidate journey duration.
First Recruiter Contact → Offer Accepted Used in sourced/pipeline hires. Better for proactive recruiting measurement.
Recruiter Screen Completed → Offer Accepted Downstream-focused. Strips out application processing lag; measures pure process speed.

Hiring Velocity
Application Conversion Rate
The percentage of applicants who progress through each stage of your hiring funnel
+

Definition & Context

Application Conversion Rate can be measured at each funnel stage or as an end-to-end ratio (applicants to hires). High-volume roles with low conversion rates signal sourcing or JD misalignment. Low-volume roles with low conversion signal process friction. Breaking this metric down by stage exposes exactly where your pipeline leaks.

How organizations measure it

End-to-End: Applicants ÷ Hires Overall funnel efficiency. Industry averages range from 1–3% depending on role type.
Stage-to-Stage: Applications → Phone Screen Identifies screening threshold calibration issues or poor candidate fit from sourcing.
Apply Start → Apply Complete Career site metric. Completion rates below 60% indicate application friction problems.

Hiring Velocity
Offer Acceptance Rate
The percentage of extended offers that candidates accept
+

Definition & Context

Offer Acceptance Rate is a late-funnel signal that reflects compensation competitiveness, candidate experience quality, and recruiter alignment with hiring managers. A declining OAR often signals a comp benchmarking problem, but it can also indicate candidate hesitation built up from a poor experience earlier in the process.

How organizations measure it

Offers Accepted ÷ Offers Extended Standard. Best-in-class organizations target 85–90%+ acceptance rates.
First-Choice Offer Acceptance Excludes counter-offers or reissued offers. Stricter measure of first-time accuracy.
Acceptance Rate by Source / Channel Identifies which sourcing pipelines yield candidates most aligned with comp expectations.

Cost & Spend
Cost Per Hire
The total investment required to successfully fill one position
+

Definition & Context

Cost Per Hire is the SHRM-standardized formula that combines internal costs (recruiter compensation, technology, overhead) with external costs (job boards, agency fees, advertising). CPH is a critical executive metric because it connects TA investment to business value, and it's often significantly underreported when technology consolidation opportunities are missed.

How organizations measure it

SHRM Formula: (Internal + External Costs) ÷ Total Hires Industry standard. Internal = recruiter time, tech stack, overhead. External = boards, agencies.
Direct Spend CPH: External Costs Only ÷ Hires Simplified version. Used when internal cost allocation is not available.
CPH by Role Type or Department Segmented view. Surfaces which requisitions disproportionately consume budget.

Cost & Spend
Agency & Third-Party Spend
The proportion of hires and budget flowing through external sourcing vendors
+

Definition & Context

Staffing agency fees typically run 15–25% of first-year salary, making agency dependency one of the highest-cost levers in TA. Tracking this metric reveals whether your internal sourcing capability is keeping pace with demand, and which roles or departments are consistently bypassing the internal process. Reducing agency reliance by even 10% can generate significant annual savings.

How organizations measure it

Agency Hires ÷ Total Hires (%) Dependency ratio. Tracks whether internal sourcing is scaling with volume.
Total Agency Spend ($) per Period Absolute spend tracking. Essential for budget forecasting and reduction goal-setting.
Job Board Spend by Source Performance ROI view. Spend-per-qualified-applicant by channel identifies which boards to deprioritize.

Productivity
Recruiter Productivity
How effectively recruiters convert time investment into qualified pipeline and hires
+

Definition & Context

Recruiter Productivity spans multiple sub-metrics: hires per recruiter, time spent per hire stage, and the ratio of strategic to administrative work. Research consistently shows recruiters spend 30–60% of their time on administrative tasks that can be automated: scheduling, status emails, posting management, and reporting. Measuring this creates the business case for technology investment.

How organizations measure it

Hires Per Recruiter Per Quarter Output metric. Industry benchmark varies by role complexity; typically 10–25 hires/quarter for corporate recruiting.
Time-on-Task Breakdown by Activity Type Process metric. Tracks hours spent on scheduling, screening, communication, reporting, etc.
Requisition Load Per Recruiter Capacity metric. Indicates whether teams are under-resourced or whether process automation is needed.

Source of Hire Effectiveness
Which channels deliver the best candidates, and at what cost
+

Definition & Context

Source of Hire tracks the origin channel of every hire: organic career site, job boards, referrals, CRM pipelines, agencies, social, and more. Most organizations know their source of application, but the high-value insight is source of hire: which channels produce candidates who actually get offers and start. This drives sourcing strategy and budget allocation decisions.

How organizations measure it

Hires by Source Channel (%) Primary view. Breaks down what percentage of filled roles came from each channel.
Cost Per Qualified Applicant by Source ROI view. Spend divided by qualified candidates (not all applicants) from that channel.
Source-to-Hire Conversion Rate Funnel view. Tracks how efficiently each source converts to interviews, offers, and hires.

Quality & Retention
Quality of Hire
A composite score measuring how well new hires perform and contribute after joining
+

Definition & Context

Quality of Hire is the most strategically important TA metric, and the hardest to measure. It connects talent acquisition decisions to downstream business outcomes. A strong QoH framework creates a feedback loop between TA and hiring managers, turning anecdotal performance judgments into structured data that improves future hiring decisions.

How organizations measure it

Hiring Manager Satisfaction Survey (30/60/90-day) Most common. Structured rating of new hire performance at key post-hire milestones.
Composite Score: Performance + Retention + Ramp Time Advanced model. Weighted average of performance rating, tenure at 12 months, and time to full productivity.
Quality by Source Channel Strategic view. Identifies which sourcing channels produce highest-performing hires over time.

Quality & Retention
First-Year Turnover
The percentage of new hires who leave within their first 12 months
+

Definition & Context

First-year turnover is a critical TA accountability metric because it reveals whether hiring decisions and onboarding experiences are setting employees up for long-term success. High first-year turnover is expensive: replacement costs typically run 50–200% of annual salary when you factor in recruiting, training, and lost productivity. It often signals misalignment in role expectations set during the hiring process.

How organizations measure it

Employees Who Left Within 12 Months ÷ Total Hires in Period Standard formula. Measured on a rolling or cohort basis.
90-Day Turnover Rate Early warning signal. Very high 90-day exits indicate onboarding or role expectation failures.
First-Year Turnover by Source / Recruiter / Role Type Diagnostic view. Surfaces patterns that point to specific sourcing or process problems.

Candidate Experience Score
How candidates rate their overall experience with your hiring process
+

Definition & Context

Candidate Experience Score is both a quality metric and a brand metric. Candidates who have poor experiences, even if not hired, share those experiences publicly on Glassdoor, LinkedIn, and with their networks. Measuring CX creates accountability for the experience you're delivering at every touchpoint and provides the diagnostic data to fix specific failure points.

How organizations measure it

Post-Process NPS or CSAT Survey Sent to all candidates, hired and not hired. Measures likelihood to recommend or reapply.
Stage-Specific Feedback Surveys Targeted surveys after application, interview, or offer. Pinpoints where experience breaks down.
Glassdoor / External Review Monitoring Passive signal. Tracks employer brand health from unfiltered candidate feedback.

Compliance
Compliance & Audit Readiness
How consistently your process meets regulatory requirements and withstands audit scrutiny
+

Definition & Context

Compliance in TA spans OFCCP/EEO documentation, disposition tracking, adverse impact analysis, and data retention. Most organizations only discover compliance gaps during an audit; by which point remediation is expensive and reputational risk is real. Proactively measuring compliance posture turns it from a reactive fire drill into a managed, auditable process.

How organizations measure it

Disposition Rate Completeness (%) All applicants properly dispositioned in ATS. Incomplete records are the most common OFCCP finding.
Time-to-Compliance Report Generation Process metric. Hours required to pull audit-ready EEO/disposition reports on demand.
Adverse Impact Analysis Score Statistical review of selection rates by protected class at each funnel stage.
